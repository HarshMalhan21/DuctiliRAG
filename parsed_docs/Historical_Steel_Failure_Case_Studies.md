# Historical Steel Failure Analysis Case Studies

## Case Study 1: Premature Rotating Bending Fatigue Failure of Plain Carbon Steel Shaft (S35C / AISI 1035)
- **Component & Material:** Industrial drive shaft made of 0.35% C plain carbon steel (C=0.35%, Mn=0.75%, Cr=0.15%, Mo=0.0%, CE=0.51), uncarburized, oil/polymer slack-quenched (THQCr=8 °C/s) and tempered at 600°C for 60 minutes.
- **Observed Symptom:** High-cycle rotating bending fatigue fracture at 380 MPa stress amplitude prior to reaching 10^7 cycles.
- **Metallurgical Root Cause Analysis:** Metallographic examination per ASTM E3 and hardness traverses per ASTM E384 revealed a soft surface microstructure (~230 HV) consisting of coarse spheroidized cementite in a recovered ferrite matrix along with slack-quenched grain-boundary ferrite networks due to low Carbon Equivalent (CE=0.51) and slow quench cooling rate. Without Cr and Mo additions, tempering at 600°C caused severe matrix softening. Additionally, quantitative metallography per ASTM E45 identified elevated Type A sulfide (dA=0.08) and Type B alumina (dB=0.05) inclusions that accelerated crack nucleation.
- **Corrective Actions:** Upgrade material to Cr-Mo alloy steel (AISI 4140 / JIS SCM440 with Cr=1.0%, Mo=0.20%) to increase hardenability (ASTM A255) and tempering resistance; increase quench agitation rate (THQCr >= 16 °C/s); or apply surface carburizing / induction hardening to introduce compressive surface residual stresses.

## Case Study 2: Subsurface Fish-Eye Fatigue Fracture in High-Strength Alloy Steel Gear Shaft
- **Component & Material:** Quenched and low-temperature tempered Cr-Mo alloy steel shaft operating under high-cycle rotating bending at 720 MPa.
- **Observed Symptom:** Sudden brittle-appearing transverse fracture at 4.2 x 10^6 cycles with no prior surface cracking detected during routine visual inspection.
- **Metallurgical Root Cause Analysis:** Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) fractography revealed a classic subsurface 'fish-eye' fracture origin located 350 microns below the surface. Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (EDS) confirmed a coarse 45-micron Type B Alumina (Al2O3) inclusion cluster (elevated dB rating per ASTM E45) combined with an insufficient hot-forging reduction ratio (RedRatio < 450).
- **Corrective Actions:** Enforce strict cleanliness specifications per ASTM E45 Method D (dB <= 0.005), utilize vacuum-degassed and calcium-treated clean steel, and increase forging reduction ratio above 700 to consolidate internal porosity.

## Case Study 3: Intergranular Fracture and Temper Embrittlement in Ni-Cr Steel Fastener Without Molybdenum
- **Component & Material:** Medium-carbon Ni-Cr steel bolt tempered at 550°C and slowly cooled, containing 0.0% Molybdenum (Mo_added = 0).
- **Observed Symptom:** Premature crack initiation and fast brittle crack propagation under cyclic bending loads.
- **Metallurgical Root Cause Analysis:** SEM fractography showed intergranular decohesion along prior-austenite grain boundaries rather than ductile transgranular fatigue striations. Auger electron spectroscopy confirmed phosphorus impurity segregation to grain boundaries during the 550°C temper cycle due to the absence of Molybdenum (Mo=0%).
- **Corrective Actions:** Specify 0.15-0.25% Mo addition (Mo_added = 1) to scavenge phosphorus and prevent grain boundary embrittlement, and water-quench rapidly after high-temperature tempering.
